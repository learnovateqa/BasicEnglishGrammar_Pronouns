Comprehensive Pronoun Masterclass Study Guide

Detailed rules, distinctions, and clear examples for grammar starters

1 Subject Pronouns vs. Object Pronouns

Subject Pronouns act as the main doer of the verb in a sentence. List: I, you, he, she, it, we, they.

Examples:
• She baked a delicious chocolate cake for dinner.
• They arrived at the conference early this morning.

Object Pronouns receive the action of the verb or follow prepositions. List: me, you, him, her, it, us, them.

Examples:
• The manager thanked us for our hard work.
• Please hand the important documents to him.
2 Possessive Adjectives vs. Possessive Pronouns

Possessive Adjectives always stand directly *before* a noun to modify it. List: my, your, his, her, its, our, their.

Examples:
• This is my favorite blue notebook.
• Have you seen their new puppy?

Possessive Pronouns replace the noun entirely and stand alone without repetition. List: mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs.

Examples:
• That blue notebook on the desk is mine.
• Our house is large, but theirs is even larger.
3 Reflexive vs. Intensive Pronouns

Words ending in -self or -selves (myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, yourselves, themselves).

Reflexive Pronouns: Used when the subject and object are identical (the subject does the action to itself).

Example: He accidentally cut himself while carving wood.

Intensive Pronouns: Used solely to emphasize a noun or subject right next to it.

Example: The director himself signed the contract papers.
4 Demonstrative & Interrogative Pronouns

Demonstrative Pronouns point to specific items. Singular: this, that. Plural: these, those.

Examples:
• This is an incredible painting.
• Those are the mountains we hiked last year.

Interrogative Pronouns are used to ask direct or indirect questions (who, whom, whose, what, which).

Examples:
• Who left the back door unlocked?
• Which flavor of ice cream do you prefer?
5 Relative Pronouns (Who, Whom, Whose, Which)

Relative pronouns connect dependent clauses back to nouns:

• Who: Used for subjects (The student who scored highest...).
• Whom: Used for objects (The candidate to whom you spoke...).
• Whose: Shows possession (The artist whose work was displayed...).
• Which/That: For things/animals (The car that broke down...).
6 Indefinite Pronouns & Subject-Verb Agreement

Indefinite pronouns refer to non-specific people or things.

Always Singular: Words like everyone, everybody, someone, nobody, anybody, each, either, neither take singular verbs.

Example: Everyone in the auditorium was silent during the speech. (Not *were*).
